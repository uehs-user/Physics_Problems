# Wave Properties: Solving for Wavelength

## The Fundamental Wave Equation

The relationship between wave speed, frequency, and wavelength is given by:

$$v = f \cdot \lambda$$

Where:
- **v** = wave speed (m/s)
- **f** = frequency (Hz = cycles per second)
- **λ** (lambda) = wavelength (m)

Rearranging for wavelength:

$$\lambda = \frac{v}{f}$$

---

## Given Information

| Symbol | Name | Value |
|--------|------|-------|
| f | Frequency | 440 Hz |
| v_air | Speed of sound in air | 343 m/s |
| v_water | Speed of sound in water | 1482 m/s |

---

## Part 1 — Wavelength in Air

### Step 1: Identify the formula
$$\lambda_{air} = \frac{v_{air}}{f}$$

### Step 2: Substitute the known values
$$\lambda_{air} = \frac{343 \text{ m/s}}{440 \text{ Hz}}$$

### Step 3: Calculate
$$\boxed{\lambda_{air} = 0.780 \text{ m}}$$

---

## Part 2 — Wavelength in Water

### Step 1: Apply the same formula
$$\lambda_{water} = \frac{v_{water}}{f}$$

### Step 2: Substitute the known values
$$\lambda_{water} = \frac{1482 \text{ m/s}}{440 \text{ Hz}}$$

### Step 3: Calculate
$$\boxed{\lambda_{water} = 3.368 \text{ m}}$$

---

## Key Concept: Why Does Wavelength Change?

> **The frequency stays constant** — it is determined by the source (the vibrating object producing the sound, e.g. a tuning fork at 440 Hz).
>
> **The wave speed changes** depending on the medium — sound travels faster through denser, more elastic materials like water.
>
> Therefore, **wavelength must increase** proportionally when the wave enters a faster medium.

$$\frac{\lambda_{water}}{\lambda_{air}} = \frac{v_{water}}{v_{air}} = \frac{1482}{343} \approx 4.32$$

The wavelength in water is **~4.32 times longer** than in air.
